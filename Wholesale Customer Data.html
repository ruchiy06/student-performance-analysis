The Channel and Region variables were excluded from the clustering dataset because they are categorical variables intended for use in the external validation stage rather than in the clustering process itself. The six numerical spending variables were subsequently transformed using the log1p function to reduce right-skewness and lessen the impact of outliers. Following the logarithmic transformation, the data were standardized using StandardScaler, which rescaled each feature to have a mean close to 0 and a standard deviation close to 1. This preprocessing step prevents variables with larger spending values from dominating the Euclidean distance calculations employed by clustering algorithms such as K-Means and Ward Hierarchical Clustering. The standardized dataset contains values centered around zero with both positive and negative values, confirming that the preprocessing steps were successful and that the data are ready for clustering.

The Elbow Method was applied to determine a suitable number of clusters by examining the relationship between the within-cluster sum of squares (inertia) and the number of clusters. The results show a continuous decline in inertia as the number of clusters increases, reflecting better separation of the data. This suggests that increasing the number of clusters beyond five offers limited improvement.Although the elbow point is relatively gradual rather than sharply defined, five clusters appears to strike an effective balance between minimizing within-cluster variation and avoiding unnecessary model complexity.

Silhouette analysis was conducted to assess clustering performance for values of K ranging from 2 to 10. The silhouette coefficient measures the degree to which observations are similar to others within the same cluster while remaining distinct from observations in different clusters. The highest score (approximately 0.29) occurred at K = 2, after which the scores steadily decreased as additional clusters were introduced, whereas larger values of K resulted in lower scores. Although the Elbow Method identified K = 5 as a reasonable option based on the rate of decrease in inertia, the silhouette analysis suggests that K = 2 provides a clearer and more distinct clustering structure. Therefore, K = 2 was selected as the optimal number of clusters for the remainder of the analysis.

The clustering performance of four different algorithms was compared using the silhouette score as the evaluation metric. Among the methods evaluated, K-Means achieved the highest score (0.2903), demonstrating the greatest degree of within-cluster similarity and between-cluster separation. GMM achieved a score of 0.2656, while Ward Hierarchical Clustering recorded a slightly lower score of 0.2585. Based on these findings, K-Means was selected for the final customer segmentation analysis. Unlike the other methods, DBSCAN did not produce a valid clustering solution with the selected parameter values. After excluding observations classified as noise, only one cluster remained, making it impossible to compute a silhouette score. This suggests that the customer data does not exhibit the density patterns that DBSCAN is designed to detect.

K-Means

The PCA visualization of the K-Means clustering reveals two relatively distinct customer clusters with only minor overlap between them. While a small number of observations lie near the boundary between clusters, the majority are grouped into clearly separated regions. Although a few points appear near the cluster boundary, the overall separation is clear and consistent with the silhouette analysis. Since K-Means also recorded the highest silhouette score (0.2903), it was selected as the preferred clustering method for the remainder of the analysis.

Ward Hierarchical Clustering

The Ward Hierarchical Clustering algorithm also identified two customer clusters that are reasonably distinguishable in the PCA visualization. Compared with K-Means, however, the clusters display increased overlap, suggesting reduced cluster cohesion and separation. This finding is reflected in the lower silhouette score (0.2585), suggesting that Ward produces slightly less distinct customer segments.

Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)

The Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) identified two customer clusters that exhibit a moderate degree of separation in the PCA projection. Greater overlap is visible near the cluster boundary because GMM models clusters using probability distributions. his behaviour is expected because GMM estimates the probability that each observation belongs to a cluster instead of assigning observations using fixed boundaries. Consequently, the algorithm achieved a slightly lower silhouette score (0.2656), suggesting that its clusters are less well separated than those generated by K-Means.

DBSCAN

The DBSCAN visualization indicates that most customers were grouped into a single cluster, while a number of observations were identified as noise (labelled -1). This pattern indicates that the chosen DBSCAN parameters were insufficient to identify distinct density-based customer groups. After removing the noise observations, only one cluster remained, preventing the calculation of a silhouette score. Overall, DBSCAN provided a less effective clustering solution than the other algorithms evaluated.

External Validation Analysis

Validation using Channel: The clustering results were compared with the existing Channel classifications to assess the extent to which the identified clusters corresponded with the known customer groups. The results show a clear association between the two classifications, with the majority of Channel 1 customers belonging to Cluster 0 and most Channel 2 customers assigned to Cluster 1. These findings demonstrate a strong alignment between the clustering results and the existing customer groups, supporting the effectiveness of the K-Means algorithm.

Validation Using Region: The cross-tabulation between Region and the K-Means clusters indicates that customers from all three regions are represented in both clusters. The results show that customers from all three regions are spread across both clusters, with Region 3 containing the largest proportion of customers. This suggests that purchasing behaviour is more strongly associated with customer characteristics than with geographic location.

Adjusted Rand Index: The Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) between the K-Means cluster assignments and the known Channel classifications is 0.5135. This value indicates a moderate degree of similarity between the clustering solution and the predefined customer groups. This suggests that the clustering algorithm identifies customer groups that broadly correspond to the predefined channels while also revealing meaningful behavioural differences not fully represented by the original classifications.
